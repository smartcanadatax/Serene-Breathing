Breathing for High Blood Pressure — Slow Your Heart Rate Naturally

May 2026 · 6 min read

← Back to Blog

⚠️ Breathing exercises are a complementary tool — not a replacement for medical treatment. If you have high blood pressure, work with your doctor. Never stop prescribed medication without medical advice.

High blood pressure (hypertension) affects 1 in 4 Canadian adults. While medication and lifestyle changes are the primary treatments, slow breathing exercises have been shown in multiple clinical studies to meaningfully reduce blood pressure — and the effect can be felt within a single session.

How Breathing Affects Blood Pressure

Your blood pressure is controlled partly by your autonomic nervous system — the same system regulated by your breath. When you breathe fast and shallowly, your sympathetic nervous system stays active, keeping blood vessels slightly constricted and heart rate elevated.

Slow, deep breathing activates the parasympathetic branch, which:

  • Widens blood vessels (vasodilation) — reducing resistance and lowering pressure
  • Slows heart rate — directly reducing blood pressure
  • Reduces cortisol — the stress hormone that raises blood pressure over time
  • Improves heart rate variability (HRV) — a marker of cardiovascular health

The Science: What Studies Show

A 2021 study published in the Journal of the American Heart Association found that slow breathing (around 6 breaths per minute) for 5–10 minutes daily reduced systolic blood pressure by an average of 9 mmHg over 6 weeks — comparable to some blood pressure medications.

The key is consistency. One session won't permanently lower your blood pressure. Daily practice over weeks creates lasting changes in your nervous system's baseline tone.

Best Breathing Technique for Blood Pressure: Resonance Breathing

Resonance breathing (also called coherent breathing) involves breathing at exactly 6 breaths per minute — 5 seconds in, 5 seconds out. At this rate, your heart rate oscillations synchronize with your breathing rhythm, maximizing heart rate variability and vagal tone.

Resonance Breathing — Step by Step:

  • Sit comfortably upright or lie down
  • Inhale slowly through your nose for 5 counts
  • Exhale slowly through your nose or mouth for 5 counts
  • No pause between inhale and exhale — keep it smooth and continuous
  • Practise for 10 minutes daily

This is the rate used in most research studies on breathing and blood pressure. It feels unusually slow at first — most people breathe at 12–20 breaths per minute. Give it 3–5 sessions to start feeling natural.

Extended Exhale Breathing

Making your exhale longer than your inhale is another powerful technique. The exhale phase is controlled by the parasympathetic system — a longer exhale means more parasympathetic activation and more heart rate slowing.

4-6 Breathing:

  • Inhale through your nose for 4 counts
  • Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 counts
  • Repeat for 5–10 minutes

When to Practise for Maximum Effect

  • Morning — blood pressure is naturally higher in the morning; breathing first thing can reduce the daily peak
  • Before stressful situations — meetings, difficult conversations, medical appointments
  • Evening — lowers blood pressure before sleep, improving overnight recovery
  • Any time you notice tension — shallow breathing and muscle tension are early warning signs of pressure rising

Combining Breathing With Lifestyle

Breathing works best as part of a complete approach. For blood pressure, combine daily breathing practice with:

  • Reducing sodium intake
  • Regular moderate exercise (walking, swimming)
  • Limiting alcohol
  • Maintaining a healthy weight
  • Following your doctor's medication plan

Breathing is not a cure — but it's a free, side-effect-free tool that genuinely works when used consistently alongside medical care.
